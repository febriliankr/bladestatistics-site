How it goes

Point at a folder, ask, read the output.

  1. Open the folder your data already lives in

    No import step and no project format. Blade reads the files where they sit and writes its output beside them.

  2. Ask the question the way you would ask a colleague

    Blade reads the variables, picks a method, and tells you what it chose before it runs anything.

    "Does the treatment group still differ on the outcome once I control for baseline score and age?"

  3. Read the result, then take it with you

    APA-formatted output, tables, plots, assumption checks, a plain-language reading of what it means, and the R syntax underneath. Export the transcript, the figures, or the code.

Why the numbers hold

The model writes the call. R computes the answer.

Nothing is estimated by a language model

A p-value in Blade came out of an R procedure, the same one you would run by hand. The model's job is to choose the procedure, pass the variables, and explain the output it got back.

When R complains, you see it

Convergence warnings, singular fits, violated assumptions: these come through instead of being smoothed over. An analysis that failed says so.

The syntax stays behind

Every analysis writes runnable R into your folder. Hand it to a supervisor, a reviewer, or a statistician and they can check the work without Blade.

Your machine, your files

Blade runs on the R and Python installed on your computer, in a folder you choose. Your data files are never uploaded. Every file write and shell command waits for you to approve it.

The hosted API does the planning and holds the model keys. It stores the session transcript, nothing from your workspace.

It does the mechanics, you own the claim

Blade is fast at the part that is mechanical and cannot decide whether your design supports the conclusion. That part is still yours.

Questions

Before you download.

Do I need to know R?

No. You ask in plain language and read the explanation. The R is there for when someone asks you to show your work, and it is real R you can run yourself.

Will it open my SPSS files?

Yes. SPSS .sav files are read through R, along with CSV, TSV, Excel, and Parquet, with variable and value labels intact.

Can I trust a language model with statistics?

Not with the arithmetic, which is why it never does it. The model selects the procedure and interprets the output; an R engine computes the result. Both the choice and the syntax are shown to you, so a wrong call is visible rather than buried.

Does my data leave my computer?

Your data files stay in your folder. Blade's hosted API plans the analysis and stores the session transcript; it never receives your dataset files. Every write and every command asks for approval first.

Is this a replacement for a statistician?

It replaces the hours spent on menus, syntax, and reformatting output. It does not decide whether your design supports your conclusion, and it will tell you when a question is outside what the data can answer.

What about Linux?

Not published yet. macOS and Windows builds are above.
